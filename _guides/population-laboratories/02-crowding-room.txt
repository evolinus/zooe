# intro
Individuals now get in each other's way: births fall and deaths rise as the population grows, and where the two meet the population stops changing. That point is the carrying capacity $K$. This guide asks what $K$ does and does not mean, and where growth is fastest.

# objectives core
- Explain how density-dependent births and deaths produce a carrying capacity.
- Describe the logistic curve and show that $K$ is approached from above as well as below.
- Locate the fastest population growth at $K$/2 and explain why.
- Explain how an Allee effect creates a threshold below which a population declines.

# objectives ext
- Use $rK$/4 to predict the maximum total growth rate, and discuss maximum sustainable yield critically.
- Compare deterministic and stochastic versions of the same model.
- Explain why $r$ sets the speed of the approach and $K$ its height.

- **Fisheries or wildlife management session:** steps 1 and 6 with question 4.

# step core 8 | The S-shaped curve
set: The defaults: $N$<sub>0</sub> = 10, $r$ = 0.40, $K$ = 400, time span 60, demographic noise off.
predict: Where will the population level off? At what population size will it be growing fastest?
run: Press **Run**.
look: the population curve against the purple $K$ line and the pale exponential, and the *Total growth rate vs. density* hump.
explain: Explain the gap between the logistic curve and the pale exponential.

# step core 8 | Starting above K
set: $N$<sub>0</sub> = **700**.
predict: What will the population do?
run: Run.
look: the *Births and deaths vs. density* panel at the start of the run.
explain: Why is $K$ called an attractor? What is happening to births and deaths above $K$?

# step core 10 | The Allee effect
set: Open *More parameters* and switch the **Allee effect** on, threshold $A$ = 40. Run with $N$<sub>0</sub> = **30**, then $N$<sub>0</sub> = **50**.
predict: What will each population do?
run: Run both.
explain: Why is being rare itself dangerous here? Give two biological reasons an Allee effect might occur.

# step core 6 | K or r?
set: Allee off. *Compare two populations*: **On**, population 1 with $K$ = 400 and population 2 with $K$ = 600, same $r$ and $N$<sub>0</sub>.
predict: Which population reaches half its own $K$ first?
run: Run.
explain: Which parameter sets how high the curve goes, and which sets how fast it climbs?

# step ext 10 | Maximum sustainable yield
set: One population, the defaults.
predict: The total growth rate $rN$(1 − $N$/$K$) peaks at $N$ = $K$/2. Calculate the peak value.
run: Run and read the top of the hump on *Total growth rate vs. density*.
explain: In the logistic model this is the largest harvest that could be taken forever. Give three reasons why setting a real fishery's quota at this value has repeatedly proved too much.

# step ext 10 | Noise near the ceiling
set: **Demographic noise**: On, **20** replicates, $K$ = **40**, $N$<sub>0</sub> = 10.
predict: Will the replicates sit exactly at $K$? Could any go extinct?
run: Run twice; then raise $K$ to 400 and run again.
explain: How does the size of the fluctuations around $K$ change with $K$, relative to $K$ itself? What does that imply for small reserves?

# step ext 8 | Allee threshold with noise
set: Noise on, 20 replicates, $K$ = 400, Allee on with $A$ = 40, $N$<sub>0</sub> = **45**.
predict: Deterministically this population just escapes. With noise, what fraction of replicates will fall below $A$ and be lost?
run: Run twice and count.
explain: Why is a population near an unstable equilibrium especially at the mercy of chance?

# questions
- [core] A deer population is held at 50% of $K$ by hunting. Is that population growing fastest, slowest, or somewhere in between? Explain.
- [core] Why does the room say $K$ "belongs to the species" only in this model? What would make $K$ change?
- [core] Give an example of a species with an Allee effect, and explain the mechanism.
- [ext] The logistic model assumes density dependence is a straight line. How would a curved relationship move the peak of the growth hump?
- [ext] Why do theta-logistic and other generalisations exist? What does the logistic get wrong for large mammals?
- [ext] Explain why "maximum sustainable yield" is a property of the model rather than of the population.

# check
- **Step 1.** The population levels off at $K$ = 400 and grows fastest at 200, the peak of the hump.
- **Step 2.** It declines smoothly to $K$: above $K$ deaths exceed births. $K$ attracts from both sides.
- **Step 3.** From 30 the population declines to extinction; from 50 it grows to $K$. Mate finding, group defence or cooperative feeding can all cause an Allee effect.
- **Step 4.** Both reach half of their own $K$ at the same time: $r$ sets speed, $K$ sets height.
- **Step 5.** $rK$/4 = 0.4 × 400/4 = 40 individuals per unit time. Real stocks fluctuate, are age-structured, and are counted with error, and their density dependence need not be linear.
- **Step 6.** The replicates fluctuate around $K$; at $K$ = 40 the swings are large relative to $K$ and some can be lost. Relative fluctuations shrink as $K$ grows.
- **Step 7.** A good share of replicates fall below 40 and are lost, though the deterministic run survives.
